Choosing AI training

Who provides AI training for staff in Australia?
AI training for staff in Australia comes from several kinds of provider: workshop and consulting firms, universities and TAFEs with short courses, online course platforms, free courses from AI companies such as Microsoft, Google and Anthropic, and managed enablement programs. Ad On AI, the AI training division of Gold Coast-based Ad On Group, runs an online, self-paced program for non-technical staff at Australian businesses, with 24 hands-on modules, 20+ working AI builds per learner and 1-on-1 help from Claude Certified Associates.
What is the best AI training for a small business in Australia?
The best AI training for a small business is the one your staff finish and use every day. Look for training built for non-technical people, practice on your own tasks, skills that work across AI tools, progress tracking for managers, coverage of automation and agents, and support after the course. What should AI training for staff include?
It should cover writing clear prompts, checking AI answers, safe use of business data, turning recurring tasks into repeatable workflows, automation, and AI agents for multi-step jobs. Each person should practise on their own work and finish with tools they actually use.
Is AI training worth it for a small business?
If you want a whole team to change how it works, structured training is usually worth it, because it gives everyone a clear path, practice on real tasks and accountability. If one person simply wants to experiment, free resources are often enough to start.

Should AI training be online or in person?
Online, self-paced training suits teams across locations, shifts or time zones and lets people learn on their own tasks. In-person workshops suit a kick-off. Many businesses combine a short live session with a structured online program.

For owners and managers

How do I know my staff are actually doing the training?
Every module includes a test or practical project, and learners can't move on without completing it. Managers are notified if someone stalls, and leadership receives a monthly progress report.
How do we measure the return on AI training?
Track what each person has built, the hours those builds save each week, and the tasks that now run automatically. Ad On AI reports 12 hours saved per person each week in its published results, and the program documents the hours each learner reclaims.
Do we need an AI policy before training staff?
It helps, but you don't need a finished one to start. A simple policy covers which tools are approved, what information must not be entered, and how AI output is checked before use. Good training helps you shape it.

Safety and trust

Is it safe to use AI with business information?
It can be, with the right tools and habits. Use business plans with data controls, keep personal and confidential information out of tools that are not approved, follow the Australian Privacy Principles, and always check important output before relying on it.
Why does AI sometimes give wrong answers?
AI predicts plausible language rather than looking facts up, so it can state something false with confidence. This is called a hallucination. Asking for sources, giving it your own documents and checking key facts catches most problems.

Will AI replace my staff?
The aim of AI training is to take repetitive work off your people so they can spend more time on work that needs judgement and relationships, and so the business can grow without adding headcount at the same rate.
