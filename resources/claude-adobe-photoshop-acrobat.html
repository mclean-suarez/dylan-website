Claude + Adobe

Use Photoshop and Acrobat inside Claude, without opening either

The prompts from the video for product photos and contracts, plus how to turn Adobe on in Claude in under a minute.

By Beau Robards, Certified Claude Expert
Published 18 November 2026 · 6 min read
The short answer

Adobe now works inside Claude. Turn on the Adobe connector and you can call on more than 80 of its tools, across Photoshop, Acrobat, Express, Premiere and more, from a normal chat. Upload a photo or a PDF, say what you want in plain English, and Adobe does the edit. To turn it on, go to Customize, find Adobe and click Connect.

What you need

  • A Claude account. Adobe says the connector works on free and paid Claude plans. Set it up on claude.ai or in the Claude desktop app.
  • An Adobe account is optional. Many tools work without signing in. Signing in unlocks more tools, higher usage limits and saving your work between chats. Some tools need a paid Adobe plan.
  • No Photoshop or Acrobat skills. You don’t need either app installed, and you don’t need to know which tool does what. You describe the result and Adobe picks the tools.

1. Turn Adobe on in Claude

  1. Open Customize. In Claude on the web or the desktop app, click Customize in the left sidebar.
  2. Find Adobe. Open Connectors and search for Adobe. If it’s not there, check under Plugins → Discover, where Adobe now lists it, and click Add.
  3. Click Connect. Sign in with your Adobe account when asked to unlock everything.
  4. Start a new chat. Upload a file and ask. You can also type /adobe to start with Adobe’s own skill.

On a Team or Enterprise plan? If Claude says your organisation doesn’t allow third-party apps, your Claude admin needs to allow the Adobe connector first.

2. Product photos with Photoshop

Upload a photo and tell Claude what you want. Start your prompt with “Use Adobe” so Claude knows to reach for Adobe’s tools rather than describing the edit to you.

These are the prompts we use for product photos. Pick a tab, copy it and fill in the parts in [square brackets].

clean-product-shot.txt
Use Adobe to edit the product photo I've uploaded so it's ready for my online store.

- Brighten it and balance the lighting so it looks natural, not over-edited.
- Straighten it if it's on an angle.
- Remove the background and put the product on plain white.
- Keep the product's real colours, shape, label and text exactly as they are.

Give me the finished image to download, and tell me in one line what you changed.
instagram-ready.txt
Use Adobe to turn this photo into an Instagram post for [business name].

- Crop it to a 4:5 portrait for the feed, with the product centred and a little space around it.
- Make a 9:16 version for Stories too, with space at the top and bottom for text.
- Brighten it slightly and soften anything distracting in the background.

Don't change the product itself. Give me both versions to download.
whole-batch.txt
I've uploaded [number] product photos. Use Adobe to edit them all the same way so they look like one matching set:

- Same brightness and white balance across every photo.
- Background removed and replaced with plain white.
- Product centred and cropped square, with the same amount of space around each one.

Keep every product's real colours and labels. Name the files [product name]-1, [product name]-2 and so on, and give me them all to download.
new-background.txt
Use Adobe to remove the background from this product photo and place the product on [a warm timber benchtop with soft morning light].

Keep the product exactly as it is, with a natural shadow underneath so it looks real. Give me 2 options to choose from, both cropped square.
website-banner.txt
Use Adobe Express to make a website banner from this product photo.

- Size: 1920 x 600 pixels.
- Headline: "[your headline]"
- Button text: "[Shop now]"
- Brand colours: [your colours or hex codes]

Put the product on the right and leave clear space on the left for the text. Give me the banner to download and open it in the editor so I can tweak the wording.

Tip: new backgrounds and other generative edits may ask you to sign in to Adobe first. If a tool isn’t available on your account, Claude will tell you and suggest what it can do instead.

3. Contracts and PDFs with Acrobat

Acrobat works the same way. Upload a PDF and ask. The one from the video, blacking out private details before you send a document, is the one most businesses use first.

redact-before-sending.txt
Use Adobe Acrobat to redact this PDF before I send it to [who it's going to].

Black out every:
- phone number
- email address
- bank account number and BSB
- tax file number
- home address
- [anything else that's private]

Don't redact [anything that must stay visible, like our business name and ABN].

Then list every redaction you made, by page, so I can check it, and give me the redacted PDF to download.
review-a-contract.txt
Use Adobe to review this contract for me. I run a [type of business] and I'm the [customer / supplier] in this agreement.

Highlight every clause about payment terms, price increases, termination, automatic renewal, liability and penalties. Add a plain-English comment to each one saying what it means for us and whether anything looks unusual.

This isn't legal advice, so finish with a short list of questions I should ask a lawyer before I sign.
combine-pdfs.txt
Use Adobe Acrobat to combine the PDFs I've uploaded into one file, in this order: [first file], [second file], [third file].

Delete any blank pages, and name the file [client name] - [document] - [date]. Give me the combined PDF to download.
split-a-pdf.txt
Use Adobe Acrobat to split this PDF into separate files:

- Pages [1 to 3]: [the quote]
- Pages [4 to 9]: [the terms and conditions]
- The rest: [the appendix]

Name each file clearly and give me them all to download.
pdf-to-word.txt
Use Adobe Acrobat to convert this PDF into an editable Word document. Keep the headings, tables, numbering and page breaks as close to the original as possible, and tell me about anything that didn't convert cleanly.

Always check before you send: open the redacted PDF and look at every page yourself, and keep your original. Details inside scanned images, signatures or handwriting are the easiest to miss. And because your file is processed by Adobe as well as Claude, follow your business’s rules on what you can upload.

4. What else it can do

Photoshop and Acrobat are the headline, but the connector reaches more of Adobe. You don’t have to name the app. Describe what you want and it picks the right tools.

PhotoshopBalance lighting, blur or remove backgrounds, straighten and crop photos, one at a time or in batches.
AcrobatRedact, combine, split and convert PDFs. Edit text, reorder pages and add comments and highlights.
ExpressSocial posts, banners and flyers, with an editor where you can change the text, colours and fonts one by one.
PremiereCrop and resize a video for YouTube, TikTok and your Instagram feed in one go.

It also reaches Illustrator, Lightroom, InDesign and Adobe Stock. It doesn’t include every feature of every Adobe app, so for detailed design work you’ll still want the full app or your designer. For the small daily jobs, it saves the wait.

FAQs

Do I need Photoshop or Acrobat installed?
No. You don't need either app on your computer. The Adobe connector runs the tools for you inside your Claude chat, and you download the finished file.
Is Adobe in Claude free?
Adobe says the connector works on free and paid Claude plans, and many tools work without signing in to Adobe. Signing in with an Adobe account unlocks more tools, higher usage limits and saving your work between chats. Some tools need a paid Adobe plan.
Where do I find Adobe in Claude?
Click Customize in the left sidebar, open Connectors and search for Adobe. Adobe now also lists it under Plugins, then Discover, where you click Add. Then click Connect and sign in.
Is it safe to redact contracts this way?
It's a fast way to black out private details, but always open the redacted PDF and check every page yourself before you send it, and keep your original. Details inside scanned images, signatures or handwriting are the easiest to miss. Your file is processed by Adobe as well as Claude, so follow your business's rules on what you can upload.
Do I have to say "Adobe" in my prompt?
You don't have to name the exact tool, because Adobe picks the right ones for the job. Starting your prompt with "Use Adobe", or typing /adobe, makes sure Claude uses Adobe's tools rather than just describing the edit.
Adobe isn't showing up or won't connect. What do I do?
Update the Claude desktop app or refresh claude.ai, then search again under Customize. On a Team or Enterprise plan, your Claude admin may need to allow third-party connectors first.
Can it do everything the full Adobe apps can?
No. Adobe says the connector doesn't include every feature of each app. It's best for quick everyday jobs like photo clean-ups, social sizes and PDF tasks. For detailed design work, use the full app or your designer.
